# ADR 0015 — How the assistant learns: memory now, models in releases

**Status:** Accepted, 8 October 2026, by the maintainers. The community's reviewers ratify it with the
governance document before the pilot. It amends the conditions of
[ADR 0003](0003-no-fine-tuning-initially.md) under which fine-tuning may begin. The second speed
(reviewed community memory) is built: "say it your way" is live and the gap list is built (8 Oct 2026). Learner
memory and the release pipeline are planned.

**Context:**

We want each community's assistant to get better from use: to learn how people speak, which words they don't
understand, and which explanations work. It should feel like assistants that "learn" (for example Nous
Research's Hermes Agent) while keeping data in the community.

Four facts shape how:

- **Hermes Agent doesn't learn by retraining itself.** Its learning loop is memory and skills. It writes
  reusable procedures, consolidates memory and models its single user. Turning sessions into training data is
  a separate, offline step.
- **Live training on chats is unsafe here.**
  - About 250 planted documents were enough to backdoor models from 600M to 13B parameters in a 2025 study.
    A Bitcoin assistant is exactly what scammers would try to teach "the agent needs your seed phrase".
  - Fine-tuned models memorise names, numbers and addresses.
  - Our own policy forbids training on conversations without opt-in.
- **Model quality is limited by validated examples, not training time.** The closest precedent (Sunbird AI's
  Sunflower for Ugandan languages) used about 3,000 instruction examples. The training itself took hours.
- **Most hosted models' outputs can't be used for training.** OpenAI's and Anthropic's terms forbid using
  outputs to build competing models. Synthetic data from models that write textbook Kiswahili would teach
  textbook Kiswahili.

**Decision:** The assistant learns at three speeds, each with its own gate.

1. **For one person, at once (planned).** A member may opt in to learner memory: their language, level and
   the topics they have covered. It is used only for them, stored on the node, and deletable by them.
2. **For the community, the same day (partly built).** Approved knowledge cards, phrases and glossary entries
   are live through retrieval as soon as a reviewer approves them. New inputs are added, and every one goes to
   reviewers, never straight to everyone:
   - **"Say it your way" (Teach mode):** a member rewrites an answer the way they would say it, and chooses
     what the rewrite may be used for (answers, evaluation, training). The choice is recorded in the existing
     `allowed_for_*` flags.
   - **Gaps:** counts of questions no card answered, shown only at 5 or more.
3. **The model, in releases (research).** An adapter is trained on approved examples whose contributors
   consented to training. A version ships only if it:
   - beats the current version on a human-labelled test set it was never trained on;
   - has zero regressions on a safety suite (scam and secret cases);
   - is no worse on a general sanity set;
   - is accepted by a vote of the community's reviewers.

   Rollback means loading the previous adapter.

**Rules that go with it:**

- **Nothing updates model weights directly from a live conversation.**
- **No synthetic training data from models whose terms forbid it.** "Teacher" models for synthetic data
  must be open-weight models with permissive licences (for example Apache-2.0 or MIT). Synthetic data may
  teach structure (answer shape, grounding on cards, saying "not covered"), not a community's voice. The voice
  comes from people.
- **Two kinds of adapter.** A **public** adapter is trained only on data consented for public release. A
  **private** adapter is trained on community-only data and never leaves that community's node, because
  weights can leak training data.
- **Each community is its own language problem.** Before launch, a community runs a language readiness check
  (its own starter questions, blind review of candidate models). The result decides whether it launches on
  day one, launches in a bridge language with community-written cards, or collects data first.

**Consequences:**

- Improvement people can see comes mostly from the second speed, and it costs reviewer time. That time is the
  limiting resource, and should be measured.
- Fine-tuning can start once three things exist: a human test set and a safety suite per community, an agreed
  training-consent policy, and enough consented, reviewed examples. ADR 0003's "not initially" becomes "not
  without those three".
- The Dataset Lab's existing synthetic batches, generated by proprietary models, stay evaluation-only.
- New work:
  - Teach mode, the gap list and learner memory.
  - Human test sets per community.
  - A training export that refuses rows without training consent.
  - A release-and-vote step in the community's console.

**Alternatives considered:**

- *Online learning from every chat:* rejected because of poisoning, privacy, consent, and the Tay precedent.
- *Synthetic data from proprietary ZDR models:* rejected because their terms forbid it, and because zero data
  retention protects the input, not the right to the output.
- *Pre-training our own base model:* rejected. It costs on the order of hundreds of thousands of dollars of
  compute for a model weaker than open ones, and there isn't enough Swahili text, let alone Sheng.
