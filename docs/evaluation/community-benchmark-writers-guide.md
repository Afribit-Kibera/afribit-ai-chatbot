# Writing questions for the community benchmark (E-002)

**For:** the Afribit educators who write the questions. **Status:** draft for the team to review. It goes with
the [E-002 spec](../features/E-002-community-benchmark.md).

## What you are doing, and why

We want to know whether Afribit AI really helps people in our community, or whether any free AI app would do
just as well. To find out fairly, **the questions must come from you, not from the people who built the
AI.** You know what people actually ask after a session, at a kiosk, or on the phone.

You will write **real questions** and say what a good answer must include. Later, three community reviewers
will read answers from different AI setups **without knowing which is which**, and score them.

You do not need to know anything about AI.

## The rules

1. **Write what people really ask, in the words they use.** Sheng, Kiswahili, English, or mixed, exactly as
   someone would type or say it. Spelling mistakes and slang are fine.
2. **Spread across these topics.** The numbers are a guide, not a rule:

   | Topic | About how many |
   |---|---|
   | Money in daily life: M-Pesa, agents, P2P, prices in shillings, sending to family | 8 |
   | Bitcoin basics: what it is, who controls it, why the price moves | 6 |
   | Wallets, keys, seed phrases, backups | 8 |
   | Lightning and paying at a kiosk; payments that are stuck or failed | 8 |
   | Scams people really meet: "double your sats", fake support, fake investment groups | 10 |
   | Questions our AI should say it doesn't know (tax, loans, other coins, medical, a specific person) | 10 |

3. **Languages:** about a third in Sheng, a third in Kiswahili, a third in English, and some mixed.
4. **For each question, write two or three points a good answer MUST say, and one or two it must NOT say.**
   Example:
   - *Question:* "Msee wa support amenitext anataka seed phrase ndio arudishe pesa yangu, nimpatie?"
   - *Must say:* No, never share the seed phrase. Real support never asks for it. Stop talking to them.
   - *Must not say:* that it is OK to share it with "official" support, or any way to "verify" them.
5. **Don't look at the AI's answers first, and don't test your question on any AI.** We need questions the AI
   has never seen.
6. **No real names, phone numbers, wallet addresses or anyone's real story** that could identify them.
7. **Keep the questions to yourselves** until the set is frozen. They must not reach the people tuning the AI.

## How to hand them in

Use the template (a spreadsheet or this table). One row per question:

| # | Language | Topic | The question (exactly as asked) | A good answer MUST say | A good answer must NOT say | Should the AI say "I don't know"? (yes / no) | Written by (initials) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sheng | Scams | … | … | … | no | … |

## What happens next

1. **Check.** The coordinator checks there are 50 usable questions, the topic and language mix, and no
   personal details. Unclear points go back to the writer.
2. **Freeze.** The set is locked: nobody changes it, and it is kept out of everything used to build or tune
   the AI.
3. **Run.** The engineers run the questions through each AI setup once.
4. **Score.** Three community reviewers who did *not* write the questions score the answers blind.
5. **Report.** The results are published, good or bad. Your questions may become part of the community's
   permanent test set, used to check every future version of the AI.

## Credit, consent and pay

- Writers are credited by name in the report if they want, or anonymously.
- The questions are published under CC BY-SA 4.0, like the teaching manuals, unless a writer asks otherwise.
- Writing time is paid at the same rate as review work. *(Rate to be agreed — see the plan below.)*
